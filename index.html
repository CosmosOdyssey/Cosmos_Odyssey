Earth's Major Systems

Explore the complex systems that make our planet unique and habitable. These interconnected systems work together to create the environment that sustains all life on Earth.

Hydrosphere

All of Earth's water, including oceans (96.5%), lakes, rivers, groundwater, and glaciers. The hydrosphere covers about 71% of Earth's surface and contains approximately 1.4 billion cubic kilometers of water.

Atmosphere

The layers of gases surrounding Earth that protect life and regulate climate. Composed of 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, and trace amounts of other gases. Extends up to 10,000 km but 75% of mass is within 11 km.

Lithosphere

Earth's rigid outer layer, including the crust and upper mantle. Divided into tectonic plates that move at 1-10 cm/year. Continental crust is 30-50 km thick, while oceanic crust is 5-10 km thick.

Biosphere

The global ecological system integrating all living organisms and their interactions. Extends from 8 km above sea level to 11 km below ocean surface. Contains an estimated 8.7 million species.

Earth's Structure

Earth is composed of multiple layers with distinct chemical and seismic properties. These layers formed over 4.5 billion years through planetary differentiation.

Crust

5-70 km thick, continental (granite) and oceanic (basalt) types. Temperature ranges from 0°C at surface to 400°C at boundary with mantle. Contains all known life and human activity.

Mantle

2,900 km thick, semi-solid rock that flows slowly over time. Divided into upper and lower mantle. Temperatures range from 500°C to 3,700°C. Convection currents drive plate tectonics.

Outer Core

2,200 km thick, liquid iron and nickel generating Earth's magnetic field. Temperatures between 4,000-5,000°C. Convection currents create geodynamo effect protecting Earth from solar winds.

Inner Core

1,250 km radius, solid iron-nickel alloy with temperatures over 5,000°C. Pressure reaches 3.6 million atmospheres. Rotates slightly faster than Earth's surface.

Earth's Vital Statistics

Dimensions

Equatorial diameter: 12,756 km. Polar diameter: 12,714 km. Circumference: 40,075 km (equatorial). Surface area: 510 million km² (29% land, 71% water).

Mass & Gravity

Mass: 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg. Surface gravity: 9.8 m/s². Density: 5.51 g/cm³. Earth is the densest planet in the solar system.

Orbit & Rotation

Orbital period: 365.256 days. Rotation period: 23h 56m 4.1s. Axial tilt: 23.44°. Average orbital speed: 29.78 km/s.

Climate

Surface temp range: -89°C to 56.7°C. Average: 14°C. Atmospheric pressure: 101.325 kPa at sea level. Earth is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface.

Our Solar System

The Sun and everything bound to it by gravity, including planets, moons, asteroids, and comets. Our solar system extends far beyond the eight planets, with the Oort Cloud reaching about 1.6 light-years from the Sun.

The Sun

Our G-type main-sequence star, containing 99.86% of the solar system's mass. Diameter: 1.39 million km. Surface temp: 5,500°C. Core temp: 15 million°C. Energy output: 3.8×10²⁶ watts.

Inner Planets

Terrestrial planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars - rocky with solid surfaces. Formed closer to Sun where only metals and silicates could condense. All have impact craters and tectonic features.

Outer Planets

Gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn) and ice giants (Uranus, Neptune). Jupiter is 318 times Earth's mass. All have ring systems and numerous moons. Contain hydrogen, helium, water, ammonia, methane.

Dwarf Planets

Including Pluto, Ceres, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris. Pluto has a diameter of 2,377 km and a thin nitrogen atmosphere. Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt.

Key Space Missions

Humanity's robotic explorers have visited every planet in our solar system and continue to push the boundaries of our knowledge.

Voyager Program

Launched in 1977, now in interstellar space, carrying the Golden Record. Voyager 1 is 23.5 billion km from Earth (2023 data). Still transmitting data from beyond the heliopause.

Mars Rovers

Perseverance (2020), Curiosity (2012), Opportunity (2004-2018), Spirit (2004-2010) exploring the Red Planet's surface. Searching for signs of past life, studying geology and climate.

Hubble Telescope

Launched in 1990, revolutionized astronomy with stunning deep-space images. Orbit: 547 km altitude. Has made over 1.5 million observations of 50,000 celestial objects.

James Webb

Infrared telescope launched in 2021, studying the early universe. Located at L2 point, 1.5 million km from Earth. Can see galaxies formed just 200 million years after the Big Bang.

Cosmic Phenomena

Black Holes

Regions of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing can escape. Sagittarius A* at our galaxy's center is 4 million solar masses. First image captured in 2019 by Event Horizon Telescope.

Supernovae

Powerful stellar explosions that can briefly outshine entire galaxies. Type II occurs when massive stars collapse. Type Ia occurs in binary systems. Distant supernovae revealed universe's accelerating expansion.

Nebulae

Giant clouds of dust and gas where stars are born. Emission nebulae glow from nearby hot stars. Reflection nebulae scatter light. Dark nebulae block light. Planetary nebulae are dying stars' ejected material.

Dark Energy

Mysterious force causing universe's expansion to accelerate. Comprises 68% of universe's total energy. First evidence found in 1998 through observations of distant supernovae.

Seven Natural Wonders

Recognized for their extraordinary natural beauty and significance. These wonders showcase the incredible diversity and power of Earth's geological and biological processes.

Great Barrier Reef

World's largest coral reef system off Australia's coast, visible from space. 2,300 km long with 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands. Home to 1,500 fish species and 400 coral types. Formed over 20 million years.

Grand Canyon

Massive gorge in Arizona carved by the Colorado River over 6 million years. 446 km long, up to 29 km wide, and 1.8 km deep. Exposes nearly 2 billion years of Earth's geological history in its rock layers.

Aurora Borealis

Natural light display in polar regions caused by solar particles colliding with atmospheric gases. Most active during solar maximum. Can extend 80-640 km above Earth's surface. Also occurs on other planets with magnetic fields.

Parícutin Volcano

Mexican volcano that suddenly appeared in a cornfield in 1943. Grew 424 m in first year. Erupted continuously until 1952, reaching 2,800 m elevation. One of few volcanoes whose birth has been observed by modern science.

Victoria Falls

Largest waterfall in Africa on the Zambia-Zimbabwe border. 1.7 km wide with 108 m drop. Local name "Mosi-oa-Tunya" means "The Smoke That Thunders." Forms largest sheet of falling water in the world during flood season.

Harbor of Rio

Stunning natural harbor surrounded by dramatic granite mountains including Sugarloaf (396 m) and Corcovado (710 m). Formed by erosion of ancient rock formations. Features iconic Christ the Redeemer statue.

Mount Everest

Earth's highest peak at 8,848.86 m (2020 measurement) in the Himalayas. Formed 50 million years ago by collision of Indian and Eurasian plates. Grows about 4 mm taller each year. Over 300 climbers have died attempting to summit.

Biodiversity Hotspots

Regions with significant levels of biodiversity that are threatened by human activities. These areas contain at least 1,500 endemic plant species and have lost at least 70% of their original habitat.

Amazon Rainforest

Largest tropical rainforest with unparalleled biodiversity. 5.5 million km² across 9 countries. Contains 10% of known species, including 40,000 plant, 3,000 fish, 1,300 bird, and 430 mammal species. Produces 20% of Earth's oxygen.

Madagascar

Island with unique wildlife found nowhere else on Earth. 90% of its wildlife is endemic. Home to all 100+ species of lemurs. Has lost 90% of original forest cover. Contains 5% of world's plant and animal species on just 0.4% of land area.

Coral Triangle

Marine area in Southeast Asia with highest coral diversity. 6 million km² across 6 countries. Contains 76% of world's coral species and 37% of reef fish species. Supports 120 million people. Threatened by overfishing and climate change.

Cape Floristic

South African region with exceptional plant diversity. 78,000 km² with 9,000 plant species, 69% endemic. Includes fynbos vegetation adapted to nutrient-poor soils and frequent fires. Home to the iconic protea flowers.

Geological Marvels

Zhangjiajie Pillars

China's towering sandstone pillars up to 1,080 m tall. Formed by 300 million years of erosion. Inspired the floating mountains in "Avatar." Contains the world's highest outdoor elevator (326 m) and glass-bottomed bridge (430 m long).

Waitomo Glowworm Caves

New Zealand caves illuminated by thousands of bioluminescent glowworms (Arachnocampa luminosa). The larvae produce blue-green light to attract prey. Formed over 30 million years in limestone bedrock.

Eisriesenwelt Ice Cave

Austrian limestone cave with 42 km of passages filled with spectacular ice formations. Largest ice cave in the world. Temperature remains below 0°C year-round. Ice forms from snowmelt that freezes in winter.

Salar de Uyuni

Bolivia's 10,582 km² salt flat, world's largest. Formed from prehistoric lakes. Contains 50-70% of world's lithium reserves. When covered with water, creates perfect mirror effect reflecting the sky.
